================================================================================
FRENTE ALIADA — GAME CONCEPT & KEY CHARACTERISTICS
"A Segunda Guerra em duas frentes" / "The war on two fronts"
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CONCEPT (ONE PARAGRAPH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Frente Aliada is a short, browser-based, educational real-time strategy game set
in World War II. The player takes command of Allied forces in a stylized trench
warfare battle, buying squads with coins, placing reinforcements, and ordering
them to advance, retreat, or hold a line. Each of the 10 missions recreates a
real historical battle in chronological order, framed by a historical briefing,
and completing it unlocks a "Diary of War" fact sheet. The goal is not to
simulate war realistically, but to make the player understand WHY each battle
mattered while playing a compact, readable trench-defense/assault game.

Elevator pitch:
  "A 2–4 minute playable history lesson: hold or break a line of trenches in a
   real WWII battle, then unlock the story behind it."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT THE GAME IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Genre:        Real-time tactics / trench defense & assault (light RTS)
- Platform:     Web browser, desktop and mobile (single HTML page, no install)
- Tech:         Vanilla JavaScript + HTML5 Canvas 2D, no libraries, no build step
- Session:      ~2–4 minutes per battle, ~30–40 minutes for the full campaign
- Tone:         Educational, respectful, non-graphic; pixel-art presentation
- Language:     Brazilian Portuguese (UI, history, and all content)
- Save:         Local only (localStorage), no accounts, no server
- Content:      10 historical battles, 5 per front, 1939–1945 framing

Core fantasy:
  You are an Allied field commander. You do not control individual soldiers;
  you control the LINE. You decide where reinforcements arrive, when a trench
  attacks, when it falls back, and whether troops stop at a friendly line or
  pass through it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DESIGN PILLARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HISTORY FIRST — Every mission is a real battle, presented in real
   chronological order, with context, stakes, commanders, outcome and a
   "Did you know?" fact. Gameplay simplifies combat so the history stays legible.
2. READABLE TRENCH COMBAT — Seven vertical trench lines, two sides, clear
   ownership colors. No fog of war, no micromanagement of individual units.
3. SHORT SESSIONS, REAL DECISIONS — 2–4 minute battles built around three
   choices: where to deploy, when to push, and when to fall back.
4. EARNED PROGRESSION — Victories unlock the next battle and a full historical
   diary entry. Medals (Bronze/Silver/Gold) reward efficient command.
5. TWO FRONTS — The campaign alternates between the Western and Eastern
   fronts, showing that Germany was squeezed from both sides.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. GAME FLOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAMPAIGN SCREEN
  - Hero panel with the next mission ("dossier").
  - Timeline of all 10 battles, grouped by year, filterable by front.
  - Locked battles must be completed in order.
  - Progress chip (x/10), front counters, sound toggle.
  - Each battle card shows a historical photo and the flag of the country where
    the battle took place (images from the Images/ folder). The Germany flag
    stays blurred until the pointer enters its own small flag box; hovering the
    rest of the card does not reveal it.

BRIEFING MODAL (before every battle)
  - Header photo of the battle (Images/ folder), with the year and front tag.
  - Date, place, front, outcome, Allies, Axis, commanders.
  - "O contexto histórico" — what happened.
  - "O que estava em jogo" — why it mattered.
  - "Seu papel na missão" — the player's fictional role.
  - Objective, difficulty selector, "Did you know?" historical fact.
  - Button: "Iniciar batalha" / "Jogar novamente".

IN-BATTLE
  1. Intro overlay: mission, objective, faction, controls, hint.
  2. Buy units with coins; choose the reinforcement line.
  3. Use per-trench order buttons: ADVANCE, RETREAT, BLOCK/PASS.
  4. Capture trenches one by one until you reach and take the last enemy line.
  5. Pause at any time (Esc/P); auto-pauses when the tab is hidden.

RESULT
  - Victory/defeat report: kills, losses, trenches captured, score.
  - Rank: Bronze, Silver or Gold (best rank saved).
  - "What this battle taught" — 3 historical takeaways.
  - Historical fun fact.
  - Actions: next battle, retry, back to campaign.

META
  - Diary of War: one fact sheet per completed battle (context + fun fact),
    illustrated with the battle photo.
  - Progress ring and completion percentage.
  - Difficulty can be changed at any time; it applies to the next battle.
  - "Erase progress" resets everything.

6. BATTLEFIELD & CORE RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LAYOUT (7 vertical trench lines, left = Allied rear, right = enemy rear):

  PLAYER SIDE                                   ENEMY SIDE
  [1]----[2]----[3]----[4]----[5]----[6]----[7]
   own    own   neutral neutral  enemy  enemy  enemy

  - Line 1 and 2 start under player control.
  - Lines 3 and 4 start neutral.
  - Lines 5, 6 and 7 start under enemy control.
  - Reinforcements spawn outside the screen and walk to the selected friendly
    line (or the most advanced friendly line if none is selected).

CAPTURE RULES
  - A trench changes owner when it contains units of only one side.
  - A friendly line with no defenders and enemy units present is lost.
  - A neutral line is taken by whoever occupies it alone.
  - First capture of a trench gives +20 coins (one-time bonus).
  - Taking the enemy's last line (7) wins immediately.
  - Losing your first line (1) loses immediately.

WIN / LOSE
  - Every battle: win by reaching and capturing the last enemy line (line 7).
  - Lose only if your first line (line 1) falls.
  - There is no time limit.

D-DAY VARIANT (battle 7 only — battle.variant = "dday")
  - The battlefield begins at sea: the left edge is the English Channel, with
    animated waves, foam and a wet-sand shoreline.
  - Starting troops arrive in landing boats, in groups of up to 4. Boats cross
    the water, beach themselves and disembark. Purchased infantry is also
    assembled into boats of 4 (mixed types allowed) that only depart when full;
    purchased tanks travel alone in much slower boats.
  - There is one visible loading boat per group, with a 2x2 square showing how
    many of the 4 slots are already filled.
  - There is only ONE trench, the final enemy line at 0.90W, defended by four
    machine gunners in concrete bunkers. The other six positions are beach cover
    (hedgehogs, rocks, sandbags and craters), not trenches.
  - Line labels: Praia, Ouriços, Rochas, Muro, Cratera, Aproximação, Trincheira.
  - Bunker MGs take 30% damage (damage x0.7), fire twice as fast and reach only
    the second position; the beach landing line is outside their field of fire.
  - Starting force: 16 soldiers — 7 riflemen, 5 SMG, 2 MG, 1 medic and 1
    anti-tank — filling four landing boats.
  - Every 10 seconds two SMG assault troops counterattack from the final trench.
  - Assault troops fire on the move at a 1.55x cooldown and keep a minimum
    advance speed of 0.6 so the landing force does not arrive piecemeal.
  - Win by destroying all four bunkers and taking the final trench; lose if the
    beach staging position falls.
  - D-Day fractions: [0.205, 0.315, 0.425, 0.535, 0.645, 0.775, 0.90];
    shoreline at 0.16W.

ORDERS (per friendly trench)
  - ADVANCE (>) — all stationed units move to the next valid line. Friendly
    lines set to PASSAR are skipped; blocked friendly lines stop the advance.
  - RETREAT (<) — all stationed units fall back to the nearest friendly line.
  - BLOQ / PASSAR — toggles whether moving troops stop at this friendly line
    (blocked) or pass through it (open).
  - Units in transit cannot fight while walking, but stop automatically a few
    metres short of an enemy charging head-on and then open fire. D-Day assault
    troops are the exception: they fire on the move at a reduced cadence.
  - Combat is automatic: units prioritize enemies straight ahead in their firing
    lane, then the nearest target in range. Assault/SMG troops also throw a
    grenade at their target every 5 seconds while shooting.

ECONOMY
  - Income: +100 coins every 5 seconds (discrete ticks).
  - Capture bonus: +20 coins per trench, first time only.
  - Start coins: 260–400 depending on the battle.
  - Unit cap: 28 player units; 28 enemy units.
  - No upkeep, no selling, no refunds.

DIFFICULTY
  Recruta  (I)   enemy HP x0.80, enemy damage x0.65, attack interval x1.18,
                 starting coins x1.15 — for learning the history.
  Soldado  (II)  all multipliers x1.00 — the default campaign experience.
  Veterano (III) enemy HP x1.28, enemy damage x1.40, attack interval x0.85,
                 starting coins x0.90 — for players who know the front.

ENEMY BEHAVIOUR
  - Both sides start with comparable numbers; the enemy is topped up to the
    player's starting strength when a mission begins.
  - The enemy buys a matching unit whenever the player buys one.
  - Each enemy loss queues a replacement; replacements arrive after a 12 s
    cooldown, one at a time.
  - Enemy reinforcements walk in from outside the screen instead of
    teleporting directly into a trench.
  - Aggression timer (13–20 s base) orders attacks; attacks become more
    frequent as the player captures lines and can open a second axis after ~30%.
  - Tank chance scales with battle progress; at most 3 enemy tanks alive.
  - The enemy keeps one unit behind when attacking with 3+ units.
  - D-Day exception: the enemy never advances, never buys tanks and never
    counter-purchases. Its spawn table is weighted toward MGs, snipers and
    mortars, and its troops hold the final line and bunkers.

7. UNITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Eight purchasable types, reskinned per faction (same stats, different names):

  KEY  UNIT          COST  HP   DAMAGE  RANGE  COOLDOWN  SPEED  ROLE
  1    Rifleman       35   74     12     1.00    1.30s    0.70   Front line
  2    Assault/SMG    50   64      7     1.00    0.55s    0.85   Close combat
  3    Machine gun    80   92     10     1.00    0.30s    0.46   Suppression
  4    Sniper         95   56     36     1.00    2.40s    0.52   Long range
  5    Anti-tank     110   78     42     1.00    2.60s    0.42   Tank killer
  6    Mortar        130   68     26     1.00    3.40s    0.34   Area damage
  7    Medic          70   60      0     1.00    1.10s    0.80   Heals allies
  8    Tank          220  330     34     1.00    1.65s    0.28   Shock/armor

  - Every unit fires up to one "trench gap" (the distance between two
    neighbouring positions), not an arbitrary per-type distance.
  - Anti-tank deals x2.6 damage to tanks; non-AT units deal only x0.45
    damage to tanks (armor).
  - Mortars deal 55% splash damage in a radius around the target.
  - Tanks deal area damage around the impact point (70% base splash, with
    falloff by distance).
  - Medics heal 9 HP to the most wounded ally in range instead of firing.
  - Tank kills cause explosions, screen shake and scorch marks.
  - Unit names by faction:
      Western Allies: Lee-Enfield, Sten, Bren, Enfield, PIAT, 3-inch mortar,
                      field medic, Sherman.
      Red Army:       Mosin-Nagant, PPSh-41, Maxim, Mosin, PTRD-41, 82 mm
                      mortar, field nurse, T-34.
      Wehrmacht:      Kar98k, MP40, MG42, Kar98k, Panzerfaust, Granatwerfer,
                      Sanitäter, Panzer IV.
      Italy:          Carcano M91, Beretta M38, Breda M37, Carcano,
                      Cannone 47/32, Brixia mortar, nurse, M13/40.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SCORING & RANKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Score = kills x35 + captures x70 + 40% of remaining coins
        + 600 if victorious - losses x12.

Rank points:
  +2  victory
  +1  low casualties (<= 8 on Soldado, <= 10 on Recruta, <= 5 on Veterano)
  +1  captured at least 3 trenches
  +1  at least 15 enemy kills
  +1  controlled at least 3 lines at the end

  Gold >= 5 points, Silver >= 3, otherwise Bronze.
  The best rank and score per battle are saved.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MOUSE / TOUCH
  - Click/tap a friendly trench to select it as the reinforcement line.
  - Click/tap the > and < buttons at the base of a trench to advance/retreat.
  - Click/tap BLOQ / PASSAR to block or open a friendly line.
  - Shop buttons at the bottom buy units (desktop or mobile).
  - Pause button in the top-right corner.

KEYBOARD
  1–8        Buy unit types in shop order
  A or Q     Advance the selected/ready line
  R or E     Retreat the selected/ready line
  B          Toggle block/pass on the selected/ready line
  Esc or P   Pause / resume
  Enter/Space Open a focused battle card on the campaign screen

10. PRESENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VISUALS
  - Entire battlefield is drawn procedurally on an HTML5 canvas in a pixel-art
    style: blocky sun, drifting clouds, distant silhouettes, craters, scorch
    marks, sandbags, barbed wire, debris, grass/snow patches.
  - Per-battle palette (sky, sun, ground, haze, accent) creates a distinct mood.
  - Biomes: field, forest, snow, desert/sand, coast/beach, urban/ruins.
  - Weather: clear, rain, snow, ash, overcast (light drizzle, grey sky, no sun).
  - D-Day visuals: dark sea with animated waves and foam, wet-sand shoreline,
    landing boats with troops aboard, concrete bunkers with MG slits, anti-tank
    hedgehogs, wrecked boats, a low grey overcast sky and a cold gloom overlay.
  - The D-Day campaign card gets an animated gold shine only while the mission
    is unlocked/playable; locked cards stay muted. Its intro overlay carries a
    highlighted callout explaining that the beach-landing gameplay differs from
    the other battles.
  - Units are hand-drawn pixel sprites in code: soldiers with rifles, SMGs,
    machine guns, sniper scopes, anti-tank weapons, mortars, medics, tanks.
  - Health bars, muzzle flashes, tracers, explosions, smoke, screen shake and
    floating coin/damage text give feedback without graphic violence.
  - Layout is responsive: canvas resizes to the window, troop slots reflow,
    and the field re-lays out around the HUD and shop dock.

GRAPHICS QUALITY
  Three presets, selectable from the monitor button in the header (opens a
  settings modal) and from quick chips on the pause screen. Changes apply
  immediately, even mid-battle, and are saved in localStorage.
    Alto  (default) — the full current visual standard: internal resolution up
                      to 2x DPR, 100% scenery, weather and particles, shadows,
                      explosion rings, screen flashes, detailed sandbags and
                      multi-layer smoke. HUD refresh ~90 ms.
    Médio          — up to 1.25x DPR, 72% scenery, 65% weather, 70% particles,
                      80% effects, shadows kept, simpler effects. HUD ~110 ms.
    Baixo          — 0.85x DPR, 42% scenery, 35% weather and particles, no
                      shadows, no explosion rings or screen flashes, square
                      particles, fewer smoke layers, sparse sandbags. HUD ~140 ms.
  Lower presets cut internal canvas resolution, scenery density, particles,
  tracers, weather flakes and per-frame HUD writes, which raises FPS on weaker
  computers while keeping gameplay identical.

AUDIO
  - Recorded samples from the Sounds/ folder: Allied and enemy rifle shots,
    machine guns, three explosion variations and three scream variations.
    The correct side/front sample is chosen for each shot; tanks and mortars
    use explosions.
  - Each unit death has a 30% chance to play one of the three scream samples.
  - Synth Web Audio effects remain for hits, reloads, UI clicks, purchase,
    capture, victory and defeat jingles, and as a fallback while samples load
    or when the game is opened directly from disk (file://).
  - Global sound toggle, persisted in the save.

ACCESSIBILITY / UX
  - ARIA labels, modal focus trap, keyboard-operable cards and buttons.
  - Clear color language: gold = player, red = enemy, gray = neutral.
  - Pauses automatically when the browser tab becomes hidden.
  - Progress is saved automatically after each victory.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. HISTORICAL / EDUCATIONAL FRAMING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Each battle includes: context, stakes, commanders, outcome, the player's
    role, objective, 3 takeaways, and a verified-style "Did you know?" fact.
  - Dates, numbers and outcomes follow the broadly accepted historical record.
  - The game explicitly states that it is an educational dramatization, that
    casualty/force numbers vary between sources, and that it does not replace
    a history book.
  - No graphic violence; combat is abstract pixel action.
  - Closing quote: "Those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat
    it." — George Santayana.

12. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ACTIVE FILES (loaded by index.html)
  index.html     Markup, SVG icon sprite, campaign/diary/about screens,
                 in-game HUD, shop dock, overlays, modals.
  styles.css     Full visual identity: military/pixel theme, typography,
                 responsive layout, animations, modals, game screen.
  battles.js     window.BATTLES — the 10 historical battle definitions:
                 dates, front, place, factions, commanders, outcome, palette,
                 scene/weather, context, stakes, role, objective, fun fact,
                 takeaways and mission tuning (coins, units, spawn table,
                 aggression, tank chance, hint).
  game.js        The whole engine (~3,000 lines): save system, campaign and
                 diary UI, briefings, difficulty, recorded + synthesized audio,
                 trench RTS simulation, enemy AI, economy, scoring/ranks, canvas
                 rendering, weather, input handling, pause/end flow.

UNUSED / LEFTOVER FILES (not referenced by index.html)
  data.js, core.js, main.js, ui.js, ui-panels.js — remnants of a different,
  unrelated game called "Market Masters" (a business/investing simulator).
  They have no effect on Frente Aliada and can be ignored or removed.

  SLOT_ROWS         6
  Save key          "frente-aliada-save-v1"

STATE MODEL
  - save:      completed battles, best rank/score, difficulty, sound.
  - G:         transient battle state (trenches, units, coins, timers,

  - Phases:    intro -> playing <-> paused -> ended.
  - Persistence only on victory; defeats do not advance the campaign.

13. WHAT MAKES IT DISTINCTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - It is a genuine playable strategy game, not a quiz with a history theme:
    the lesson is delivered through the mission briefing and debrief.
  - The "line of trenches" abstraction removes RTS micromanagement and keeps
    each decision legible in seconds — ideal for classroom or short breaks.
  - Advancing through history IS the progression system: the next battle is
    literally the next page of the war.
  - Strong, cohesive art direction achieved with zero image assets: palettes,
    procedural scenery and code-drawn pixel sprites.
  - The Block/Pass mechanic creates real tactical texture: you can let troops
    flow through a line, or use a line as a dam to mass forces.
  - Honest educational framing: clear disclaimer, no glorification, and a
    "what this battle taught" recap after every mission.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. QUICK SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Frente Aliada = 10 real WWII battles + 7 trench lines + 8 unit types +
2 fronts + 3 difficulties + Bronze/Silver/Gold ranks + a historical diary,
played in 2–4 minute bursts in the browser, with pixel-art canvas battles and
synthesized sound. The D-Day mission adds a unique amphibious beach landing with
boats, bunkers and a defensive enemy line. Simple to learn, historically
grounded, and built to make the player ask "why did this battle matter?"
